# broadcast_001 — The weekly's OBS pack: four scenes, a lower-third and a stinger, in the magazine's language, driven by URL params

**Status:** v1 for the cards (signals; Todd, 2026-11-04: "I love it … Let's do the weekly 16:9 first … pull the trigger on tickets"). · **Author:** CW. · **Reads with:** `docs/of-tomorrow-brand-guidelines.md`, `src/styles/magazine.css` (the live tokens: paper `#F4ECD9`, paper-light, paper-dark, ink `#1C1A16`, poppy `#C5372C`, teal `#1F6E7A`, mustard `#D9A21B`; `--font-display` Big Shoulders Display, `--font-serif` Source Serif 4), `tests/brand.test.ts` and `tests/tokens.ts` (a token checked at its source), signals rule 27 (lo-fi, hi-fi, build), rule 26 (a walked card's Done carries its walk). Reference frames: `specs/assets/broadcast-001/reference/ref-01…16.png` (a YouTube essayist's setup Todd likes — the *flow*, not the look).

## 0. What Todd wants, in his words

A room for recording the weekly and more frequent shorts, OBS with a few scenes, and branding to put in them. The reference setup, which "is so simple, but it flows really well": (1) full-screen camera with him in the right third, an occasional zoom in for emphasis; (2) a partial zoom plus a left 45/55 split with titles and lists on the left; (3) a full-screen title, list or stylized progress indicator — "I love when they start the list on the left and then go to full screen", and when the camera stays as a small vertical rounded-corner tile of the zoomed shot; (4) an older video (black and white) or an infographic animation in a framed window. Plus lower-thirds and the standard pieces. Content comes in as URL params so he updates what OBS shows by editing the URL. Weekly 16:9 first; shorts 9:16 later.

## 1. The claim

**Every overlay is a browser source**: an HTML page with a transparent background, rendered by OBS as a layer over the camera. So the pack is a small static site under `broadcast/` in this repo, styled from `magazine.css`'s tokens, published at `broadcast.oftomorrow.net` (and usable as local files offline). The brand test covers it: a token changed in `magazine.css` turns a broadcast page's test red the same day. The show looks like the site because it is the site's CSS.

**The reference is a flow, and the flow is four layouts and the moves between them.** We keep the flow — camera, split, full, framed — and replace the look (black vignette, white Montserrat) with ours: a magazine page. Ink text on paper where there is text; the camera uncovered where there is a face; one accent colour per segment, never three at once.

## 2. The pieces (16:9, 1920×1080)

Each is one page under `broadcast/`, taking its content from the URL. Params are repeated in §3.

1. **`camera.html` — the frame.** Transparent, so the camera shows through; OBS holds the camera itself (full, or the "zoom" transform Todd keys). What the page adds: a masthead strip top-left in Big Shoulders — **OF TOMORROW** — with an issue line beneath in serif (`Todd Sampson · No. 12 · November 2026`), a segment badge (`Field Notes`, `The Ledger`, `Ask Me Anything`) in the segment's accent, and a 2 px ink rule 24 px inside the frame edge with faint paper grain in the corners. Nothing in the right third where Todd sits. This frame stays on in every scene below.
2. **`split.html` — the 45/55 split.** The left 45 % becomes a paper panel (`--paper`, with the magazine's halftone at 6 % opacity in the panel's lower-left) with a 2 px ink rule on its right edge; the camera sits in the right 55 % (OBS crops/zooms the camera; the page draws nothing there). On the panel: a kicker in Big Shoulders (the segment), a title in Big Shoulders at 72 px, and a list in Source Serif 4 at 34 px, numbered `1.` `2.` `3.` in the accent, or bulleted with an ink square. Lines arrive one at a time on a key (`?reveal=3` shows three; the dock in card 2 changes it live) with the reference's motion: a 220 ms rise of 12 px and a fade, no blur.
3. **`full.html` — full-screen title / list / progress.** The whole frame goes paper. Three modes by param: `mode=title` (one line, Big Shoulders at 140 px, ink, centred, with a thin mustard rule beneath); `mode=list` (the numbered list at 44 px, left-aligned on the magazine's 1120 px column, revealed one at a time); `mode=progress` (the reference's timeline as a magazine "plan of the issue": a horizontal ink rule, steps as ticks alternating above and below, the current step in the accent, done steps in ink, upcoming in `--caption`). **The camera tile**: when `tile=1`, a 300×420 rounded-corner (24 px) window in the lower right with a 2 px ink border and a paper-dark shadow, where OBS places the zoomed camera; the page draws the border and cuts the hole. **The move from split to full** (Todd's favourite): the same list, so `split.html` and `full.html` share one list component and the same param set; in OBS the two scenes crossfade 300 ms and the list stays put because its left edge and type size are the same in both.
4. **`framed.html` — the framed window.** A paper matte with a centred 1440×810 window (ink border 2 px, rounded 12 px, a caption line beneath in serif: `From the archive · 2024` or the infographic's title) where OBS places a media source, a browser source or a screen capture; `bw=1` adds a CSS grayscale + slight contrast filter *in a transparent region* only where OBS supports it, else the media source carries OBS's own colour-correction filter and the page only frames. The camera tile from (3) applies here too (`tile=1`).
5. **`lower.html` — the lower-third.** A caption plate on the rule: a tab in the accent (mustard by default) that slides in along the frame's bottom rule from the left, 0.5 s, holding a headline in Big Shoulders (40 px) and a subline in serif (26 px) on paper, with a small `No. 7` marker in the tab. `?title=&sub=&n=`; `?out=1` slides it away. Used for names, chapter marks, "link in the description".
6. **`stinger.webm` — the transition.** Rendered from `stinger.html` (card 3): the OT mark on a paper fold that wipes left-to-right over 700 ms, revealing the next scene at the fold's edge, with a halftone dot wipe at the trailing edge; a WebM with alpha (VP9) made with Playwright frames + ffmpeg, plus a 700 ms paper-turn sound (`stinger.ogg`, optional). OBS's stinger transition points at the file; the cut point is at 350 ms.

## 3. The URL contract (one place, `broadcast/README.md`, and a test that each page honours it)

Common: `issue` (number), `date` (YYYY-MM-DD, rendered "November 2026"), `host` (default `Todd Sampson`), `segment` (text), `accent` (`poppy|teal|mustard`, default by segment: Field Notes = teal, The Ledger = mustard, AMA = poppy), `reveal` (how many lines are shown), `tile` (`1` shows the camera tile), `theme` (`paper|ink` — ink is the inverse, for a dark segment; paper is the default).
Text: `title`, `kicker`, `sub`, `n`; lists as `l=First line&l=Second line` (repeat `l`), or `list=` a base64url JSON array for long ones; progress as `step=Cut mistakes&step=Cut waffle&now=2`.
Data (card 4): `src=<url>` fetches JSON (`{ title, items: [{ text, n?, done? }] }`) so the list can come from the ledger's API (this week's landings, a rock's steps) without typing. Everything that can be typed in the URL can also be set from the dock (card 2) — the dock writes the same params into the page through storage, so one contract.

## 4. Design language, written down (so lo-fi and hi-fi can be judged)

- **Paper where there is text, camera where there is a face.** No black vignette. The reference's dark look is replaced by `--paper` with the halftone and grain the magazine pages use; the camera is never covered by paper except the split's panel and the full-screen's matte.
- **Type:** Big Shoulders Display for everything that is a heading (masthead, kicker, title, numbers), Source Serif 4 for everything read as a sentence (lists, sublines, captions). No third face. Sizes are fixed per piece above; nothing scales with the text length — a title that does not fit is a title to shorten (the page shows a red underline in the dock preview, never on air).
- **Colour:** ink on paper; one accent per segment for the tab, numbers, the current step and the rule under a title; `--caption` for what is past or secondary. Never two accents in one scene.
- **Motion:** rises and fades of 200–300 ms, the stinger's fold at 700 ms; no blur-in (the reference's blur, ref-06, is the one thing we do not copy — it reads as rendering lag), no bounce, no parallax. The list *stays put* between split and full.
- **The masthead is the brand**, not a logo in a corner: OF TOMORROW in Big Shoulders at 28 px, letterspaced 0.12 em, with the issue line; `logo1.png` is used only in the stinger.

## 5. Cards (rule 27: lo-fi, then hi-fi, then the build)

1. **Lo-fi** — the five pages as static HTML at 1920×1080 with placeholder content in the URL, the magazine tokens, no animation, no dock; screenshots of each (`assets/broadcast-001/01-lofi-*.png`) and a one-page `broadcast/README.md` saying how to add each as an OBS browser source (local file, 1920×1080, "shutdown source when not visible" off, custom CSS empty). Todd puts them in OBS in the room and says what moves.
2. **Hi-fi + motion + the dock** — the reveals, the slide-in, the split→full continuity, the camera tile, `theme=ink`; an OBS custom browser dock (`dock.html`) with the segment, issue, title, list editor, reveal +/−, lower-third in/out, that drives every open page through storage; a test per page that the URL contract renders (Playwright, screenshots compared); the brand test extended to `broadcast/`.
3. **The stinger and the framed scene** — `stinger.html` → `stinger.webm` (alpha) + `stinger.ogg` with the render script in the repo (`npm run broadcast:stinger`), `framed.html` with the media window and the b&w treatment, and an **OBS scene collection** (`broadcast/obs/of-tomorrow-weekly.json`) that imports the six scenes with the browser sources, the camera transforms (full / zoom / tile) and the stinger preconfigured — so a new machine is one import.
4. **Publish + data** — `broadcast.oftomorrow.net` (Vercel, this repo, `/broadcast` as static), `src=` JSON lists, and two feeds from the ledger behind a read-only route: this week's landed cards by node, and a rock's steps for `mode=progress` — the show reading the business the way the dashboards will.
